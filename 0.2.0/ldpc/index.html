    LDPC constraint¶

    ldpc.sparsest_basis_stats computes, for each batch element, the exact minimum bottleneck generator weight w* (the smallest w such that the stabilizer elements of weight at most w generate the group) and the maximum qubit degree of a sparsest basis, by bit-packed GF(2) elimination over the enumerated group for n - k <= 14 (about 4 ms at n = 9), with a greedy pairwise reduction as the bound for larger codes. The environment takes ldpc_w_max with three modes: terminate (episode ends on a violating state with ldpc_violation_reward), mask (the violating action reverts to a no-op; feasible_action_mask() gives agents the mask to apply to logits), and final (only success is gated). ldpc_penalty is the soft form for the reward. w_max = 3 provably excludes every 5-qubit distance-3 code and empirically every 7-qubit one the search reaches; w_max = 4 admits [[5,1,3]] 1 and Steane 2 and excludes Shor's code 3 (w* = 6).

    Above n = 9 the penalty form fails (experiments/scaling/REPORT.md): the normalized Knill-Laflamme 4 term pays 1/630 to 1/1128 per detected error against 0.25 per unit of w* excess, and PPO converges to sparse groups of distance 1. ldpc.LdpcObjective(n, k, d, w_max, form=...) is the reward and success criterion of an LDPC-constrained target in one of four forms (ldpc.LDPC_FORMS), all built from the same base terms (Knill-Laflamme term over the Paulis of weight below d, padding penalty) with the exact w* evaluated only on candidate states (every error detected, no idle qubit) and the greedy bound elsewhere:

    form reward on every state success
    penalty normalized base minus scale * excess (the regression's form) reward 0
    unnormalized one unit per undetected error and per weight-1 stabilizer, minus scale * excess, the sum divided by the initial state's undetected count; one undetected error costs 1 / scale units of w* excess reward 0
    final the normalized base alone (hard final-state constraint) candidate with exact w* <= w_max
    schedule the normalized base until activate(), then the penalty form (the trainer switches once the success rate at the target distance reaches a threshold) any candidate before the switch, reward 0 after

    regress.LdpcSpec(w_max, mode="objective", form=...) puts one of them on a regression target (make_reward returns the objective, env_kwargs hands its success method to the environment as success_fn); the n <= 9 targets keep mode="penalty". experiments.ldpc_scaling compares the forms on [[12, 2, 3]] and [[16, 4, 3]] with w_max = 4 on the all-to-all and the square graph, three seeds each, equivariant policy, 1.5M env steps or 20 minutes per run (experiments/ldpc_scaling/REPORT.md, runs in experiments/ldpc_scaling/runs/). Result (36 runs plus 4 scale variants, 0.62M to 1.5M env steps each on a shared machine): the unnormalized form is the default (ldpc.DEFAULT_LDPC_FORM). It learns distance where the penalty form never did (61 to 69 percent of terminal states at distance 3 on five of six [[12, 2, 3]] runs, 64 percent on the best 4x4-lattice seed) and it is the only form that produced codes within the bound: 3 of 12 runs, all [[12, 2, 3]] (303k and 354k env steps all-to-all, 950k on the lattice, 23 to 31 gates, w* = 4), each a single episode after which the policy keeps producing distance-3 codes with w* 5 to 6 (n = 12) or 7 to 8 (n = 16). The final form reaches distance 3 (14 to 68 percent at n = 12) and never a sparse code (0 of 12); the schedule form switches its penalty on at 0.63M to 0.91M env steps on [[12, 2, 3]] and then falls into the penalty form's distance-1 trap (0 of 12); at [[16, 4, 3]] all-to-all no form reaches distance 3 in the budget. Pricing the excess at 0.5 or 1 unit per error instead of 0.25 costs distance and buys no w*. So the constraint above n = 9 is half fixed: the distance-1 trap is gone and the bound is reachable, but the sparsity part is not yet a property of the trained policy.

    experiments.ldpc_sparsity takes the sparsity part further (experiments/ldpc_sparsity/REPORT.md, runs in experiments/ldpc_sparsity/runs/; the 12-qubit cells, [[16, 4, 3]] pending): a dense per-step term in reward units (LdpcObjective(form="shaped", shaping=c), c = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) traps at distance 1 like the penalty form; a count-unit terminal signal (form="terminal": any candidate ends the episode with 1 - h / (n - k), h the heavy generators of the sparsest basis, ldpc.heavy_generator_count) is learned fast but optimizes h rather than w* and collapses onto one dense code; the action mask ldpc.greedy_action_mask (two-qubit gates whose resulting greedy w* bound exceeds w_max are masked out of the logits) makes every terminal state feasible by construction and, on all_to_all:12, still learns distance 3 as the policy's typical output on three seeds (68 to 91 percent of terminal states at distance 3 over the last 25 updates, 769 to 2,248 distinct feasible codes per run) while trapping at w* = 4, distance 1 on square:3x4; the mask on top of the terminal reward (mask_terminal, seed 0) does it on both cells (95 and 98 percent at distance 3, all at w* = 4). The mask costs 1.3 to 2 times the wall time per env step. The default form is unchanged. On the all-to-all graph at n = 16 and 20 (--device cuda, batch 1024, 3M env steps) the same variant never reaches distance 3 (the report's scaling section), and on square:4x4 at batch 256 neither: the greedy bound over-masks as n grows (36 to 63 percent of the two-qubit actions at the end). The exact feasibility test in the mask (greedy_action_mask(method="exact"), --mask-method exact; the greedy bound as a pre-filter, then the exact rank test on the pairs it rejects, n - k <= 15) does not change that at n = 16 in 3M env steps on three seeds per graph: on all_to_all:16 it admits 4 to 7 percent of the rejected moves (the greedy bound was nearly exact) and the policy reaches one feasible [[16, 4, 3]] in 9M env steps; on square:4x4 it admits 12 to 30 percent and every run ends at distance 1. The mask costs 0.6 s per env step on the all-to-all cell (27 percent of the wall time) and 0.08 s on the lattice (the report's "Exact feasibility mask" section).
